# Blog images — visual direction options for the founder's taste pass
(PM prep, 2026-08-31; Asana 1218031790266337. Nothing here is built —
this page exists so the taste pass is a 10-minute read, not a meeting.)

Ground rules carried in from the task: site's own visual language
(Kettle v5 palette, Day/Night tokens, hearth glyph lineage), original
artwork only, no stock photos, no real faces, nothing that reads as
surveillance (no cameras, dashboards, graphs-of-a-person). Alt text
and captions obey every copy law. Static assets, webp, lazy-loaded,
no external hosts, no scripts (201).

## The three image types on offer

**T1 — Sheet preview.** A small, honest render of the actual printable
(the one-page emergency sheet, the normal-day sheet) shown at an
angle or flat, in-palette frame. Doubles as a download nudge where
the article already links the resource. Cheapest to produce (we
render our own PDFs), most "true".

**T2 — Structural diagram.** The article's own skeleton drawn simply:
topic 8's call-order ladder; article 19's first-hour / first-week /
permission layers. In-palette, few words, every word law-checked.
Earns its place only where the article HAS structure.

**T3 — Warm spot illustration.** Small scene in the kettle's world —
the kettle itself, a kitchen table, a phone on a counter, a
refrigerator door with a page on it. Sets tone, carries no claims.
The hero image lineage; needs the most taste supervision.

## Production notes for whoever builds

- One consistent illustration style across all posts — pick it once
  on the first asset, then it is the house style (same process as
  the hearth glyph: founder adopts an exact mark).
- Widths: content-column width, max ~720px display, 2x asset, webp.
- Letters genre (post #1) stays austere: one image, placed after the
  opening, never breaking the letter's voice with a diagram.
- Sheet previews must show the REAL current sheet — a preview that
  drifts from the shipped PDF is a lie the reader can print.

## What the founder rules on

(1) Which rows above survive; (2) the illustration style, chosen by
adopting one produced sample; (3) whether previews of sheets carry a
small caption (proposed: none — the surrounding paragraph already
says it).
