## The sheet

Page 1 is written for whoever comes to help and is exactly one page, the
File-of-Life convention: Who lives here (name, born, lives alone, languages,
hearing/sight/mobility notes) · Getting in (address, door code, who has a
key, building/front desk) · Health (conditions, allergies, four medication
rows, where the full med list is kept) · People to call (three) · Doctor and
pharmacy · Also good to know (pets, preferred hospital, where insurance
papers are kept).

Page 2 is for the adult child: fill it in while nothing is wrong; the fridge
is the convention because responders check it; keep a copy yourself for when
you're the one making calls; update it when medications change (a sheet
listing last year's medicines looks current and isn't); one line for where
the bigger papers live, and a filled/updated date.

## Notes for the PM

**No printed phone numbers anywhere.** Every number on the sheet is one the
family writes in. That keeps this asset clear of the researcher's five
outstanding verifications and outside the dated-footer requirement.

**Field style changed for this sheet.** Inline labels (label left, line
following on the same baseline) instead of label-above-line: denser, and the
only way thirty-odd fields fit one page at 198's type sizes. Assets 1 and 2
keep their look; if you want the family to match, say so and I'll regenerate
them on sheetlib.

**"Born" not "Date of birth", "Preferred hospital" not "Hospital
preference"** and similar: labels are trimmed to survive inline layout at
10.5pt. Flag any that read as too terse.

advance") and the article links back from its "have this ready" section.
