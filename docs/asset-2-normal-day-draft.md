## PAPER REGISTER — page 1, the sheet

**Title:** What does a normal day look like?

**Frame line:** Know what normal looks like. Notice when it changes.

### A normal morning
- Awake by ____________  ·  Out of the bedroom by ____________
- Breakfast around ____________  ·  First thing they do ____________

### A normal day
- Lunch around ____________  ·  Naps? ____________
- Out of the house on ____________________________
- Speaks to, most days ____________________________

### A normal evening
- Dinner around ____________  ·  In bed by ____________
- The evening usually looks like ____________________________
- Up in the night? ____________________________

### A normal week
- Days that are different, and why ____________________________
- (second line)
- Who comes to the house, and when ____________________________

### The phone
- Usually answers between ____________  ·  Rarely during ____________
- The phone lives ____________________________

### For them, this would not be normal
Four lines. Prompt above them, small:
"Not 'a fall'. Things that would actually be out of character: no answer by
lunchtime two days running. Missing Friday prayers. Not calling back at all."

## PAPER REGISTER — page 2, how to use it

**Fill it in on a good week.** A baseline written during a bad month records
the bad month. If they've just come out of hospital, wait until things have
settled and write down what settled looks like.

**Fill it in with them if they'll have it.** Most parents are fine with this
one, because it isn't a list of things they can't do any more. It's a
description of their life, and they are the expert on it. If they'd rather
not, fill it in from what you already know and don't make it a negotiation.

**One different day is a different day.** People go out, sleep badly, skip
lunch. What this sheet is for is the second week of something, not the first
afternoon of it.

**Give it to whoever might need it.** A sibling who visits twice a year, the
neighbor with the spare key, whoever would answer the phone if you couldn't.
The sheet is worth the paper it's on only if the person who needs to know
what usual looks like has seen it.

**Write the date, and rewrite it once a year.** Normal moves. A baseline from
three years ago will tell you something has changed when what actually
changed was three years passing.

**The last box does the work.** Everything above it is description. Those
four lines are the ones that turn "I have a feeling something's off" into a
sentence you can act on, and the one to read first when you have that
feeling.

## Notes for the PM

**Why page 1 has no intro.** 198 judges this asset on whether it gets kept,
not on search, and a sheet gets kept when the useful part is the part you can
see. Every explanatory sentence I wrote for page 1 pushed a field onto page 2,
so the explanation went to page 2 instead and page 1 became the artifact. If
you want a line of orientation on page 1, the frame line is doing that job and
I'd rather not add a second.

**The frame line is yours, verbatim.** "Know what normal looks like. Notice
when it changes." It sits under the title as the only non-field text on the
page.

**Vocabulary.** Clean in full, paper rule: no monitor, track, alert, elderly,
seniors, and no "ordinary" anywhere. The sheet's whole subject is the word
"normal", which is the one 192 kept.

**"Friday prayers" in the prompt.** It's an example of a specific weekly
fixture, and I picked one that isn't the American default on purpose, since
the reader might be writing about a parent in Chennai or Lagos as easily as
Ohio. Change it if you'd rather the examples stay neutral, but I'd keep a
non-generic one in there.

**Gating: free, no email box.** 198 calls this the "flagship/email asset" and
195 says only the flagship planner bundle collects email. A single worksheet
is not the bundle, so I have built it free with no email. If you meant this
sheet to be the thing that asks for an email, it needs a landing page and that
is a different build, so rule before it ships.

**No web page, per 198's `[paper]` tag.** But it still needs a URL to be
downloaded from and linked to, so it lives at /resources/normal-day/ with a
thin page whose job is to describe the sheet and hand over the two PDFs. That
is the minimum that lets the register, the sitemap and the other pages point
at it. Tell me if you'd rather it be a bare file with no page at all, in which
case it comes off the register and the sitemap loses a URL.

**What links to it.** Nothing yet. 198 has it as the thing other pages link
to, so once you have ruled on this draft I would add a line to asset #1's
guidance page and to article topic 8, both of which reach the point where
"you'd need to know what usual looks like" is the next thought.
