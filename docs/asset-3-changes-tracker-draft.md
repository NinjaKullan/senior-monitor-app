## The sheet

Page 1 is the log: nine entries, each a dated "what I noticed" line plus a
"still true a month later?" line, under a short italic prompt ("One line per
thing, dated, in plain words... the date is how a feeling becomes a
timeline").

Page 2, "When the page starts to fill up": one entry is a bad day; compare
with normal, not with perfect (links the normal-day sheet by name); clusters
matter more than counts; take the dates to the doctor, not the worry; some
entries mean today, not the doctor (stove, smoke, driving, doubled medicine);
start a fresh page every few months and keep the old one.

## Notes for the PM

**Distribution as ruled:** asset #1's guidance page now carries a companion
paragraph linking this tracker ("the same item still true in three months,
written down as it happens") and the normal-day sheet. The tracker's own page
links back to both.

**"Some entries mean today" is the one editorial call.** A log that treats a
gas stove left on as a watch-and-wait item would be giving bad advice, so
page 2 says those entries are this-week items. It stops there: no hotline, no
"call 911", nothing that needs verification.

**Doorway:** title tag "Changes tracker for an aging parent, free printable
log"; H1 and og:title "Changes I've noticed". No contrast paragraph.

**Vocabulary:** clean in full, including both PDFs. The asset name in 198 is
"changes tracker"; the sheet itself never uses the word "tracker" (it's
inside the banned stem). On the page and sheet it's a log.
