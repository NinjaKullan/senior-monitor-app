## Rulings that now govern this work (DECISIONS 193/194)

1. **Two vocabulary registers (DECISIONS 194).**
   - Paper rule: printable interiors, the planner, emails — full copy
     laws, no monitor/track/alert/elderly/seniors, ever. A sheet on a
     parent's fridge never describes them as being watched.
   - Google rule: web pages may use searcher vocabulary ("elderly
     monitoring") ONLY in contrast position — naming the category
     being replaced, never describing Kettle. Kettle is only ever "it
     asks nothing of them."
   - Consequence for you: keyword targets USE the real search terms;
     proposed page titles and copy must respect the register rules.
     Tag every proposed title as [paper] or [web].
2. **Gating**: individual printables are free downloads, no email;
   only the flagship planner bundle collects email. Rank assets
   accordingly (SEO value matters more for singles, perceived value
   for the flagship).
3. Never describe how Kettle works. No signals, data, phones, or
   automation. The only differentiator ever named: it asks nothing of
   the parent.
4. No invented statistics, people, or quotes. Every claim in your
   report carries a source URL. If you can't source it, mark it
   UNSOURCED rather than dropping it silently.
5. Dementia/wandering assets (items 47–50): DEFERRED from the launch
   set. Keep the research, build nothing on it yet. Nothing we ship
   may imply routine change means cognitive decline.
6. Nothing here is medical or legal advice; advance-care and financial
   content stays at values-conversation level. Collect the disclaimer
   patterns the source orgs themselves use.

## Task B — keyword and SERP reality check

The round-1 "Top 10" ranking is intuition. Ground it:

- For each of the 10 proposed assets and the 20 proposed article
  topics: the real queries people type (include the uncomfortable
  vocabulary — that is what the Google rule exists for), rough volume
  tier (high/medium/low is fine; exact counts not required), and who
  holds the top 5 organic results today (name the domains: AARP,
  NCOA, A Place for Mom, DailyCaring, care.com, etc.).
- For each: what format wins the SERP (listicle, PDF landing page,
  calculator, video) and whether a small independent site plausibly
  can rank. Kill-or-keep recommendation per asset.
- Identify 5 queries where the current results are weak or generic —
  our best early wins.

## Task D — competitor lead-magnet scan

  be clearly better-designed to bother.

## Task E — format norms for the printables

- Anything known about what actually gets printed and kept on
  refrigerators vs downloaded and forgotten.
- The flagship planner is pitched at 20–30 pages; sanity-check that
  against what comparable planners run.

aim under 800 lines. Do not draft any blog posts or printable copy —
that is the writer's job, and it happens after this report is
reviewed.
