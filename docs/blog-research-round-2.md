8. **Round 1 missed the referral layer and it is the most useful thing in section C.** The directories exist and are excellent; what does not exist anywhere is a one-page "who do I call for what" crosswalk.
9. **Format norms:** US Letter, print-only, 1–2 page singles. Nobody in the free field ships fillable PDFs — that is our cheapest visible quality signal. 20–30 pages for the flagship checks out.
10. **First three builds stay #1, #2, #3** as the PM directed, but reordered by evidence: build #6 alongside them. Target queries: "elderly parent living alone checklist", "emergency information sheet for elderly parent template", "how often should I check on my elderly parent".

## A.2 Gating quote 1 — NIA reuse (VERIFIED)

Verbatim, on all three NIA worksheet pages:

> "You can download, print, and copy the worksheets."

Sources: <https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/caregiving/caregiver-worksheets>,
<https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/advance-care-planning/advance-care-planning-worksheets>,
<https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/medical-care-and-appointments/talking-your-doctor-worksheets> — the
last with the fuller lead-in: "For your convenience, use these worksheets to organize your questions,
family and medical history, and information when talking with your doctor. You can download, print,
and copy the worksheets."

**Caveat, and it matters.** That sentence licenses *download, print and copy*. It does not on its face
license *adaptation, restyling or redistribution under our own design*. NIA's broader policy (general
public may reproduce NIA website information without permission except where a document states
otherwise) was reported from the OutreachPro help page but **the authoritative wording at
nia.nih.gov/about/policies was not fetched verbatim — UNSOURCED**. Someone should pull that page and
quote it before we build anything that adapts rather than links. The standing attribution line from
195 ("Content informed by resources from the National Institute on Aging.") remains right either way.

## A.3 Gating quote 2 — CDC STEADI (VERIFIED, but not where round 1 said)

The STEADI patient-resources page carries **no public-domain statement**. It says only that
customizable brochures "can easily be edited and tailored for your organization."

The public-domain wording lives on CDC's agency materials policy page:

> "Most of the information on the CDC and ATSDR websites is not subject to copyright, is in the public
> domain, and may be freely used or reproduced without obtaining copyright permission."

Source: <https://www.cdc.gov/other/agencymaterials.html>. Same page states the exceptions: contractor-
developed materials, licensed third-party content, and state or local government works.

**Consequence.** "STEADI is public domain" is true by inheritance from CDC policy, not by a STEADI-page
statement, and the contractor/third-party exception means it is true *for most of it*. For any specific
STEADI artefact we lean on, check that artefact for its own notice. Round 1's claim survives; its
citation does not.

## A.4 CFPB co-branding (STANDS, with conditions)

On the guides page: "Want to share the Managing Someone Else's Money guides with customers and
clients? Learn how to co-brand the CFPB booklets."
<https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/managing-someone-elses-money/>

From the co-branding instructions PDF
(<https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_msem_cobranding_instructions_2022.pdf>), verbatim:

> "Licensee must not make any changes to the content of the materials without the CFPB's express
> written permission."

Reported but not quoted verbatim (**UNSOURCED wording**, confirm before relying): logo max 1.5"×0.5",
bottom-left of cover, 300dpi CMYK. It is **self-service** — organisations apply their own logo to the
downloaded PDF. No quantity limit or ordering process found. The offer is CFPB-only; there is no
equivalent from NIA or CDC.

**Judgement.** Co-branding is available and cheap, but it puts the Kettle mark on a document whose
register we do not control, and the no-alteration rule means we cannot bring it inside the paper rule.
Recommend we **link to MSEM rather than co-brand it**, at least until the library has its own
established look. Flagging as a PM decision, not making it here.

# C. The referral layer (round 1 missed this entirely)

Federal sources are presumptively public domain and safe to quote; the nonprofits are rights-reserved
and get linked, never republished — consistent with 195's "never rebrand nonprofit PDFs".

**Eldercare Locator (ACL/HHS)** — the federal front door to local aging services. Free.
**1-800-677-1116**, <https://eldercare.acl.gov/>. Also chat, email and text. Reuse: federal, presumptively
public domain; **no explicit policy statement found on the site — UNSOURCED for exact wording**.
Weave into: #9 (safety net), #6 (emergency sheet), the local-help one-pager.

**Area Agencies on Aging / USAging** — ~600+ local agencies that "provide objective information,
advice, counseling and assistance," arranging nutrition, transport, caregiver support and home and
community-based services. <https://www.usaging.org/about>. Information and referral is typically free;
direct services are often sliding-scale — **UNSOURCED on exact fee structure**, and it varies by agency,
which is itself the caveat worth telling readers. Site is "Copyright © 2026, USAging" — link, don't lift.
Weave into: every asset that says "get local help". This is the answer to "who do I even call".

**211 (United Way)** — nationwide local-resource helpline, free and confidential ("every call is
completely confidential"), <https://www.211.org/>. "© Copyright 2026 United Way Worldwide. All Rights
Reserved." — nonprofit, link only. Caveat: locally franchised, so depth varies by region.

**NCOA BenefitsCheckUp** — "a free, confidential online tool that helps older adults and people with
disabilities find benefits programs they might be eligible for" — SNAP, Medicare Savings Programs,
Part D Extra Help, QMB and more. <https://benefitscheckup.org/>. Time to complete **not stated —
UNSOURCED**; don't promise "ten minutes". Nonprofit, link only.

**VA caregiver support** — Caregiver Support Line **1-855-260-3274**, Mon–Fri 8am–8pm ET,
<https://www.caregiver.va.gov/>. Two tiers: **PGCSS**, general support open to caregivers of any enrolled
Veteran (skills training, coaching, peer support, referrals), and **PCAFC**, the comprehensive programme
with a stipend, which carries eligibility requirements including a service-connected disability
threshold. Federal. Weave into: a sidebar on any asset, not a whole page — it is a minority of readers
but a decisive one for them.

**Medicare and SHIP** — 1-800-MEDICARE and medicare.gov. **The number itself was not confirmed on a
live medicare.gov page this pass — UNSOURCED; verify before it goes on a printable.** SHIP gives free,
unbiased one-on-one Medicare counselling with a local-office finder: <https://www.shiphelp.org/>,
**877-839-2675**. Note SHIP's national site claims "©2026 ... All Rights Reserved" despite federal
funding — link, don't lift.

**Falls prevention** — ACL's National Falls Prevention Resource Center, housed at NCOA, maintains a
programme database and the Falls Free CheckUp:
<https://acl.gov/programs/falls-prevention/national-falls-prevention-resource-center-and-resources-available>,
<https://www.ncoa.org/article/evidence-based-falls-prevention-programs/>. **Whether a true ZIP-code
class locator exists was not confirmed — UNSOURCED.** Local classes are usually arranged through the AAA.

**Alzheimer's Association helpline** — **800-272-3900**, free, 24/7/365, live staff, 200+ languages:
<https://www.alz.org/help-support/resources/helpline>. Rights-reserved; link only. Relevant to the
deferred set — keep it in the referral one-pager regardless, since a helpline number is not a claim
about anyone's cognition.

**ADRCs** — "No Wrong Door" single-entry-point centres (ACL/CMS/VA), free, all income levels:
<https://acl.gov/programs/aging-and-disability-networks/aging-and-disability-resource-centers>.

## C.1 Recommendation on the local-help asset

**Build it, and build it as a crosswalk, not a directory.** The directories already exist, are federally
maintained and are better than anything we would make. What does not exist is a plain-language,
one-page **"who do I call for what"** — problem in the left column ("she fell", "can't afford the
medications", "I need a break", "he's a veteran", "I don't understand the Medicare letter"), the single
right number in the right column. Everything that ranks today is either a single-purpose landing page
or a long roundup article. AARP and NCOA both publish resource-roundup articles; neither is print-ready.

Two constraints on it: lean on the federal sources for any reused phrasing, and **verify every phone
number against its live official page on the day the file is generated** — a printable with a stale
number is worse than no printable. Recommend a dated "numbers checked" line in the footer, and a
calendar reminder to re-verify.

# E. Format norms for the printables

**Page size.** US Letter, universally, across NIA, CDC, CFPB, AARP and alz.org. None offers an A4
variant; none addresses international printing at all (**UNSOURCED** that anyone designs for it).
Recommendation: Letter, with margins generous enough that A4 auto-scaling doesn't clip anything.

**Fillable vs print-only.** Nothing in the free field ships as an AcroForm. NIA, CDC STEADI, CFPB MSEM,
AARP and alz.org are all static print layouts with ruled lines and boxes for handwriting. Fillable PDFs
appear as a *paid* differentiator on Etsy. So: **ship both** — a print-only version for the fridge and a
fillable one for the sibling who lives 900 miles away and works in a browser. This is the cheapest
credible way to look better than everything else on the SERP.

**Length norms, with what could actually be verified.**
- Single checklist: **1 page** — e.g. alz.org's 10 Warning Signs checklist (<https://www.alz.org/asian/downloads/10_ws_checklist.pdf>).
- Worksheet: **1–2 pages** — alz.org's 10 Warning Signs worksheet; NIA's single-topic worksheets.
- Guide: multi-page. **Exact page counts for CFPB MSEM and AARP Prepare to Care could not be verified this pass — robots and PDF-binary limits blocked the fetch. UNSOURCED.** Worth one manual open each before we cite a number anywhere.
- Paid caregiver planners/binders: live Etsy listings marketed as 20–40+ page bundles for a few dollars (e.g. <https://www.etsy.com/listing/4354070635/caregiver-binder-printable-elder-medical>, <https://www.etsy.com/listing/1273927975/caregiver-planner-medical-binder-records>). Exact counts per listing unverified.

**Legibility for an older reader — the numbers, verbatim.** From NIA/NIH, "Making Your Printed Health
Materials Senior Friendly"
(<https://ccids.umaine.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/26/2011/11/NIA_Print_Materials_Senior_Friendly.pdf>):

> "Make type size at least 12 point, 13 point, or 14 point."

with 16 or 18 point advised for low-vision audiences; and:

> "Use dark type against a light background or use black type on a white background. Avoid patterned backgrounds."

> "Keeping lines from 50 to 65 characters long can help the eyes scan across the text more easily."

> "Align text to the left margin."

This is the single best-sourced design constraint we have, and it comes from NIA — the same body whose
material we already attribute. Adopt it as the house rule for print and cite it when anyone asks why
the sheets look the way they do. CDC's older-adult health-literacy page reinforces the plain-language
principles but yielded no comparable point-size numbers this pass.

**PDF accessibility.** Public-facing PDFs are expected to meet Section 508, which in practice means
WCAG-AA-equivalent: tagged structure, correct reading order, alt text, real text rather than scanned
images, labelled form fields, document title and language. Sourced to practitioner guides rather than
the regulation itself this pass — **treat as good-practice consensus, not verbatim law**.

**What gets printed and kept — honest answer: we don't know.** No controlled study was found comparing
retention of printed versus downloaded caregiver materials. **This is a genuine evidence gap; do not let
it turn into a confident claim in any copy.** What does exist: File of Life fridge-magnet programmes are
real and actively promoted by EMS agencies across US municipalities (e.g.
<https://www.sedgwickcounty.org/ems/ems-file-of-life/>,
<https://www.jocogov.org/newsroom/file-life-magnet-can-help-paramedics-during-home-emergency>) — which
establishes that emergency responders themselves treat fridge-posted information as a working practice,
and is a strong design precedent for asset #6. A 2025 JCO Oncology Practice piece argues for the
enduring value of print in patient education
(<https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/OP-25-00739>) — title-level signal only, not read in full.

**Flagship at 20–30 pages: confirmed reasonable.** It sits between AARP's guide and the 20–40 page paid
Etsy binders. Long enough to justify an email, short enough not to become the thing nobody finishes.
