# Free Digital Resources for an Aging-in-Place / Independent Living Website

For a passive monitoring service focused on older adults and independent living, **NIA (National Institute on Aging)** is one of the strongest sources of free, practical materials.

## Strong Free Resource Sources

### National Institute on Aging (NIA)

NIA has a useful collection of downloadable worksheets that fit naturally with an **aging-in-place / independent-living / passive monitoring** audience.

Their caregiver worksheet library includes resources such as:

- Home safety checklist
- Medication tracker
- Important documents checklist
- Caregiving responsibility worksheet
- Care provider hiring checklist

They state that these worksheets can be downloaded, printed, and copied.

Resource:
- https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/caregiving/caregiver-worksheets

### NIA Doctor Visit Worksheets

Useful materials include:

- Medication tracker
- Family health history
- Questions and concerns for a doctor visit
- Appointment preparation worksheets

Resource:
- https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/medical-care-and-appointments/talking-your-doctor-worksheets

### NIA Advance Care Planning Worksheets

Useful for families planning ahead for health and caregiving decisions.

Topics include:

- Health care proxy
- Treatment preferences
- Care providers to involve
- Conversation planning

Resource:
- https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/advance-care-planning/advance-care-planning-worksheets

### FEMA Older Adult Emergency Preparedness Materials

Useful topics include:

- Emergency kits
- Medication lists
- Caregiver and provider contact information
- Transportation planning
- Evacuation planning

Resource:
- https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_hm-preparedness-for-older-adults_english.pdf

### Alzheimer's Association Resource Library

Useful categories include:

- Home safety
- Emergency preparedness
- Wandering
- Medication safety
- Driving
- Caregiver tools
- Future planning

Resource:
- https://www.alz.org/professionals/health-systems-clinicians/for-patients-caregivers/downloadable-resources

# Copyright and Reuse Notes

NIA is part of the federal government. Government-produced NIA materials are generally public domain unless a specific item says otherwise.

However, do **not assume that everything on every `.gov` site can automatically be rebranded**.

Federal websites may include:

- Third-party copyrighted images
- Licensed illustrations
- Partner-produced content
- Protected agency logos or trademarks

Government logos should also not be used in a way that implies endorsement.

A safer attribution approach is:

> Content informed by resources from the National Institute on Aging.

Then create your own:

- Layout
- Branding
- Wording
- Explanations
- Checklists
- Worksheets
- Visual design

Useful government copyright guidance:
- https://www.usa.gov/government-copyright

NIA outreach information:
- https://outreachpro.nia.nih.gov/helpdesk.html

# Recommended First Flagship Download

Instead of starting with a generic printable calendar, create something highly relevant to your ideal customer.

## The Aging-in-Place Family Planner

**Suggested positioning:**

> The Aging-in-Place Family Planner — Free Printable 25-Page PDF

Possible sections:

- Monthly calendar
- Medication list
- Medical appointments
- Emergency contacts
- Home maintenance checklist
- Neighbor contacts
- Daily routines
- Preferred hospital
- Preferred pharmacy
- Family check-in schedule
- Emergency plan
- Important documents inventory
- Care provider contacts
- Transportation information
- Home safety notes
- Changes I've Noticed log

The **Changes I've Noticed** page can create a natural transition into passive monitoring.

For example:

> Instead of relying only on check-ins, passive monitoring can help families notice changes in everyday routines.

This makes the planner useful for:

- SEO traffic
- Email lead generation
- Adult children caring for aging parents
- Older adults living independently
- Families exploring aging-in-place solutions

It also attracts visitors who are much closer to the target audience for a passive monitoring service than people searching for generic calendars.

# Suggested Content Strategy

Build a collection of practical resources around four core themes:

1. **Independent Living**
2. **Family Check-Ins**
3. **Home Safety**
4. **Emergency Preparedness**

Use free government and nonprofit resources as research and inspiration, then create original branded versions that are easier to use and visually consistent with your website.

A strong next step is to research **30–50 government and nonprofit senior worksheets, planners, checklists, and guides**, organize them by topic, and identify the **10 strongest assets to recreate for SEO and lead generation**.
