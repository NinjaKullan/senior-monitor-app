SHIP: free Medicare counselinggovernment
State Health Insurance Assistance Program connects families to a trained local counselor who compares Medicare options one on one, at no cost.
Who: Anyone on Medicare, or a family member calling on their behalf, in any state.
Cost: Free. Counselors do not sell plans and are not paid by any insurer.
Why now: Medicare's open enrollment window runs October 15 through December 7, 2026, when SHIP appointments fill up fastest.
Link: https://www.shiphelp.org/

Free help choosing Medicare plans

Every state has a free, unbiased Medicare counseling program called SHIP. A trained local counselor will sit down with your parent, or with you on their behalf, and walk through the options before the December 7 deadline. No products are sold and no insurer pays the counselors.

Eldercare Locatorgovernment
A federal call center and directory that connects families to local services for a parent living alone: transportation, meals, benefits help, and home care, for the area where they live.
Who: Anyone looking for aging services for themselves or a family member, anywhere in the US.
Cost: Free. Call 800-677-1116, Monday to Friday, 8am to 9pm ET, or search online.
Why now: evergreen
Link: https://eldercare.acl.gov/

One number for local help

The Eldercare Locator is a free federal service that connects you to the Area Agency on Aging near your parent, for transportation, meals, benefits, and home care questions. Call the number or search by zip code. It is a starting point, not a company trying to sell anything.

https://eldercare.acl.gov/

Find benefits your parent may be missing

BenefitsCheckUp is a free tool from the National Council on Aging. Answer a few anonymous questions about age, income, and location, and it lists the public programs your parent might qualify for, from utility help to prescription costs. Nothing is shared and nothing is sold.

https://benefitscheckup.org/

Home delivered meals for your parent

Meals on Wheels America's locator finds the meal delivery program near your parent by zip code. Most local programs serve adults 60 and up, and cost is often free or a sliding scale based on need. It is a simple way to make sure your parent has food without a delivery app or a credit card.

Every so often I come across something that would have saved me an afternoon, or a parent a headache, so here are five worth knowing about right now.

Medicare's open enrollment window opens October 15 and closes December 7, and every state has a program called SHIP that will sit down with your parent, or with you on their behalf, and go through the options for free. Nobody is selling anything. If your family has been meaning to look at whether Mom's plan still makes sense, this is the season to make the call before the appointments fill up.

If you don't know where to start looking for help near your parent, the Eldercare Locator is a single federal phone number and website that routes you to the right local office, for meals, transportation, benefits, or home care. It's free, and it exists so you don't have to guess which local agency to call.

BenefitsCheckUp, from the National Council on Aging, is a short anonymous questionnaire that checks your parent's age, income, and zip code against more than two thousand public programs and tells you what they might actually qualify for. Ten minutes, no name required, and often the answer is a program nobody in the family knew existed.

If your parent has trouble getting to the store or cooking the way they used to, Meals on Wheels has a locator that finds the program serving their zip code. Most programs are free or based on what a family can pay.

And if your parent wants to video call the grandkids or figure out online banking but doesn't want to ask twenty times, Senior Planet runs free classes, live online and at centers around the country, built for people starting from zero.

That's five. Pass along whichever one your family needs this week.
