Every so often I come across something that would have saved me an afternoon, or a parent a headache, so here are five worth knowing about right now.

Medicare's open enrollment window opens October 15 and closes December 7, and every state has a program called SHIP that will sit down with your parent, or with you on their behalf, and go through the options for free. Nobody is selling anything. If your family has been meaning to look at whether Mom's plan still makes sense, this is the season to make the call before the appointments fill up.

If you don't know where to start looking for help near your parent, the Eldercare Locator is a single federal phone number and website that routes you to the right local office, for meals, transportation, benefits, or home care. It's free, and it exists so you don't have to guess which local agency to call.

BenefitsCheckUp, from the National Council on Aging, is a short anonymous questionnaire that checks your parent's age, income, and zip code against more than two thousand public programs and tells you what they might actually qualify for. Ten minutes, no name required, and often the answer is a program nobody in the family knew existed.

If your parent has trouble getting to the store or cooking the way they used to, Meals on Wheels has a locator that finds the program serving their zip code. Most programs are free or based on what a family can pay.

And if your parent wants to video call the grandkids or figure out online banking but doesn't want to ask twenty times, Senior Planet runs free classes, live online and at centers around the country, built for people starting from zero.

That's five. Pass along whichever one your family needs this week.
