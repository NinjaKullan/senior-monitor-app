# Kettle strip: style bible and cast

This text is pasted into every image prompt. Keep it short and literal.

## Style
Classic newspaper comic strip. Clean confident ink line, hand-lettered capitals, flat colour, no gradients, no
photo textures, no 3D. Palette only: warm paper #F7F1E8 background, warm ink #403C36 lines and lettering, one
accent green #297A5C (used sparingly: the kettle, a scarf, a mug), pale grey-green for shadow. No true black or
white. Panels have thin ink borders with a little gutter of paper between them. Faces are simple: dot eyes,
one-line mouths, expressive eyebrows. Understated humour; the feeling is relief, never alarm.

## Cast (draw exactly these, every time)
SAM, the adult child, about 45. Medium build, short dark hair with a little grey at the temples, round glasses,
a plain crew-neck sweater with rolled sleeves. Lives far away; usually seen at a kitchen counter, a desk, or on
a train, phone in hand. Warm, a bit rumpled, thinks a lot.

JUNE, the parent, about 74. Slim, upright, silver hair in a short practical cut, cardigan over a collared shirt,
reading glasses pushed up on her head. Capable and busy: gardening gloves, a crossword, a library book, a
watering can. Her iPhone is an ordinary one that sits on the kitchen table or in a cardigan pocket. She is never
frail, confused, or waiting by a window.

THE KETTLE, a small stovetop kettle in accent green with a curved spout. It appears somewhere in most strips as a
quiet background object. It never has a face, never talks, never glows or beeps.

## What must never appear
Cameras, screens full of charts, graphs, meters, rings, scores, red lights, alarms, sirens, hospital or medical
items, pill bottles, walkers, wheelchairs, nurses, worried hand-wringing, a parent slumped or lost. No app
screens at all (only a plain phone, face down or showing a blank lit screen). No real people, no logos other
than the tiny heykettle.com signature.

## Lettering rules
Straight apostrophes only. No em dashes. The word "normal", never "ordinary". Never these words: monitor,
monitoring, track, tracking, alert, alerts, surveillance, elderly, seniors, senior citizens, aging parents,
dashboard, sensor, detect, safety device. Twelve words or fewer per bubble.
