What does a normal day look like?

Know what normal looks like. Notice when it changes.

You can only notice a change if you know what it changed from. This is one page for writing down what a normal day looks like for your mother or father now, while things are steady: when they're usually up, when they eat, which days are different, when they answer the phone.

It takes about ten minutes and it is easiest to fill in with them, because it isn't a list of things they can't do any more. It's a description of their life, and they're the expert on it.

The last box is the one that does the work. Four lines for what would genuinely be out of character, written now, while nothing is wrong. Months later, when you have a feeling something's off and can't say why, that's the box you read first.

Print version (PDF, 2 pages) · Fillable version (PDF, 2 pages)

Page one is the sheet. Page two is how to use it, and what counts as a change.

Give the filled-in copy to whoever else might need it: a sibling who visits twice a year, the neighbor with the spare key, whoever would be answering the phone if you couldn't. A baseline only one person has seen is a baseline that helps on the days that person is available.

Rewrite it once a year. Normal moves, and a sheet from three years ago will tell you something has changed when what changed was three years passing.
