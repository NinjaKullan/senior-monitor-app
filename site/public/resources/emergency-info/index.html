In case something happens

One page for whoever comes to help. Print it, fill it in, put it on the fridge.

If something happened at your parent's home and a stranger came to help, what would they need to know in the first five minutes? Who lives here, how to get in, what conditions and medicines matter, who to call. This is one page that answers all of it, filled in by hand, kept where someone helping would look. Most families use the refrigerator door, and the people who come to help are used to checking there.

Emergency crews in many towns run programs built on exactly this idea, a single sheet posted at home, because it works. This one is written for a parent who lives alone, with the questions a far-away son or daughter gets asked on the phone in mind: the door code, who has a key, the pharmacy, where the insurance papers live.

Print version (PDF, 2 pages) · Fillable version (PDF, 2 pages)

Page one goes on the refrigerator. Page two is for you, and it's the version you read from when you're the one making calls.

Fill it in while nothing is wrong. It's ten minutes on a calm afternoon or an impossible job during a frightening one, and most of it your parent knows cold. Keep a copy yourself: if you ever have to ask someone to go and knock on the door, the address, the conditions and who-has-a-key are exactly what you'll be asked for, and reading them off a sheet beats remembering them while frightened. There's a companion piece on this site about that hour, what to do when a parent doesn't answer the phone, and this sheet is the half of it you can do in advance.

Update it when the medications change. A sheet listing last year's medicines is worse than no sheet, because it looks current.
