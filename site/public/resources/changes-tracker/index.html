Changes I've noticed

Write it down when you notice it. Read it back before you act.

The hardest thing about noticing a change in a parent is that memory is terrible at exactly this job. Was that the second time the stove was left on, or the fourth? Did the missed calls start before the winter or after? By the time you're worried enough to act, the details that would tell you whether to act have blurred into a feeling.

This is one page for writing things down as you notice them: a date, a plain sentence, and a note a month later on whether it's still true. It pairs with the okay-living-alone checklist, which tells you what's worth noticing, and the normal-day sheet, which is what you're comparing against.

Page one is the log. Page two is what to do when it starts to fill up.

The payoff comes at the doctor's office. "Since March: two falls she didn't mention, the stove twice, weight down" is a history a doctor can act on. "I'm worried about my mother" is a feeling they can only nod at. A filled page, read aloud, is the first version.
